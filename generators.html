Backup power that starts automatically

A standby system detects an outage, starts the generator, and transfers the connected loads after it stabilizes. When utility power returns, it switches back.

That helps if you're away from home or don't want to wheel out a generator in bad weather. There is a brief interruption during the transfer. Equipment that needs uninterrupted power needs its own backup plan.

Size it around what you need

We start with the appliances and systems you want to run. Motors, well pumps, and air conditioners can need extra power to start. Square footage alone won't tell us which generator to install.

We can plan for essential circuits or broader home backup. Load management can keep selected large appliances from running together when necessary.

Fuel and maintenance

Natural gas avoids tank refills while the supply remains available. Propane needs enough storage and a plan for refilling. We check the fuel requirements for the selected generator and site.

Both options need maintenance. Long outages still require shutdowns and checks according to the generator's manual. A gas connection doesn't mean the generator can run unattended indefinitely.

Use a portable generator with your panel

We install an outdoor inlet and a compatible interlock so you can connect a suitable portable generator. The interlock prevents the main breaker and generator breaker from turning on together.

You choose which circuits to run within the generator's capacity. A 50-amp connection doesn't mean you can run every light, outlet, and appliance at once.

We check the whole setup

  • Your panel and the interlock that fits it
  • The generator's running output, connections, and neutral arrangement
  • Which appliances need power and how much they draw
  • A safe place to operate the generator and the route to the inlet

Gasoline, propane, or natural gas?

Some portable generators accept all three. Output and runtime vary by fuel and model. We can discuss a compatible generator and coordinate a natural gas connection with a gas contractor when the site allows it.

Portable generators stay outside

Run a portable generator at least 20 feet from the house, with its exhaust pointing away from buildings and openings. Never run it in a garage or shed. Follow its manual and keep working carbon monoxide alarms in the home.
