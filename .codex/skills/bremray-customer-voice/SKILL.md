# Bremray customer voice

Write like an electrician explaining the job to a local customer. Be useful, specific, and easy to understand. Do not perform a folksy persona or deliberately add mistakes to sound human.

## Start with the customer's decision

Identify what the reader needs to know: whether Bremray does the job, what affects the cost, what information is needed, or what happens next. Answer the relevant question directly. Do not force every piece of copy into the same sales funnel.

Installations are the business priority: generators, EV chargers, and panel upgrades. Keep service calls available without giving them equal promotional weight on a general landing page. On an actual repair page, answer the repair customer's question fully.

Start with the problem that brought the customer to Bremray and the result they need. Explain what the work will solve and what happens next. Equipment brands and credentials belong in supporting information when relevant, not the opening sales pitch. For panel work, Brent says most jobs use Square D, Siemens, and Eaton, but the customer usually cares about solving the issue rather than choosing a brand. Keep the SPAN credential at the bottom as secondary information; do not present SPAN as the default recommendation.

## Accuracy comes before style

- Treat the existing website as wording to preserve where useful, not independent evidence that a claim is true. Brent has identified inaccurate certification claims in it.
- Separate offering installation from manufacturer certification, installer training, authorization, and dealer status. None implies the others. Ask Brent before adding or broadening certification claims; use the exact confirmed designation.
- Owner-confirmed wording, 2026-10-19: **Generac Aligned Partner** and **SPAN Certified Installer**. Use these exact designations. Generac Aligned Partner does not establish authorized dealer status; do not substitute “authorized dealer.” Bremray is NOT a certified Tesla Wall Connector installer. Do not claim certification for Tesla, ChargePoint, Square D, Siemens, or all major brands. Brand logos requested for the footer do not establish certification or a partnership; do not invent badges or imply additional endorsements.
- Brent also confirmed the older claims about electrical work since 1988, serving Ann Arbor since 2004, being bonded, hundreds of EV charger installations, a one-year workmanship guarantee, and the existing same-day service/two-day installation wording. Preserve their original scope. Do not turn those timing statements into a new unconditional scheduling promise or broaden the guarantee.
- Do not invent projects, customers, reviews, neighborhood experience, years in business, warranties, prices, timing, or availability. Retain relevant established wording only if it does not conflict with newer facts; flag unsupported consequential claims for owner confirmation.
- Keep uncertainty explicit where it affects a decision. Pricing depends on the actual scope and approved business rules. Never turn an illustrative estimate into a firm quote or make an unconfirmed timeline sound guaranteed.
- Keep private pricing and customer records out of public copy unless Brent specifically authorizes their publication. Do not advertise “free quotes” as a generic hook; Brent explicitly rejected that wording.

## Editing approach

Read the existing passage and surrounding page when available. If only excerpts are provided, work from those; do not invent missing context or broaden access just to find it. Brent's original copy is the baseline. Preserve its meaning, useful explanations, technical distinctions, and wording. Leave clear passages alone. Do not substantially rewrite his copy, reorganize a page, or replace a paragraph just to impose a uniform voice. When versions differ, compare with the original or live copy before treating recent assistant-written text as Brent's work.

For a voice cleanup, edit the particular phrase or sentence that causes the problem. Prefer removing padding to replacing it with new sales language. A request to look for “AI footprints” calls for a close wording review, not a wholesale rewrite or a claim that a detector can establish who wrote it.

Prefer active voice, short sentences, ordinary verbs, and contractions where natural. Name who does what: “We install the charger” rather than “The charger installation is carried out by our team.” Keep complete conversational sentences and vary their length when that helps the explanation. Do not force fragments, rigid sentence limits, or a clipped rhythm to sound human. Explain electrical terms when a customer needs them; retain details that help the customer decide.

Use everyday English without talking down to the reader. Assume an intelligent customer who does not work as an electrician. Explain the practical consequence of a technical detail when it matters; do not start with a lesson in electrical terminology or replace useful specifics with vague reassurance.

Cut decorative geography, vague praise, and generic slogans. Examples to reconsider: “proudly serving,” “unique electrical needs,” “expertise and care,” and “from historic homes to new construction.” These are editing signals, not a mechanical banned-word list. Place names belong in clear service-area information or a real, confirmed job example. Do not assume a house needs an upgrade because of its age or neighborhood.

Read the draft as an answer on the phone. If a sentence mainly makes the business sound impressive, replace it with useful information or remove it. Avoid making every section a rhetorical question, a slogan, or a three-part list. SEO can use accurate service and town names naturally without padding every paragraph.

Keep reference-photo credits compact. Brent prefers attribution and source details at the bottom of the page instead of repeated disclaimers beneath the photos. Retain required credit and license information, and never present sourced examples as Bremray's own work.

## Calibration and review

His latest instruction is: “Don't make any substantial changes to the existing copy that I had before.” He wants “active sentences, short sentences” that “sound like a normal human.”

Rejected example: “From the historic homes in Burns Park and the Old West Side to new construction in Pittsfield Township.” It decorates the paragraph and implies local experience without providing useful evidence.

Proposed rewrite, NOT yet owner-approved wording: “We install EV chargers, generators, and electrical panels in Ann Arbor. We also handle repairs and service calls.” Use as an illustration of concrete language, not a mandatory template or proof of Brent's exact voice.

Before delivering, check factual claims separately from tone. For a copy review, show focused before-and-after edits, explain the specific padding removed, and label proposed wording as a proposal rather than an approved voice sample. Briefly identify facts that need Brent's answer separately; do not quietly replace an uncertain claim with a new promise. If facts are missing, omit the uncertain claim from new copy or ask a focused question; continue independent editing. Do not ask for approval on every routine wording choice.

When Brent corrects a passage, capture the underlying preference and a short approved example. Do not treat assistant-generated wording or silence as owner approval.
