What do grounding and bonding do?

They do different jobs. Both matter, but a ground rod alone won't make faulty wiring safe.

Grounding connects the system to earth

Ground rods and other grounding electrodes help limit voltage from lightning and other electrical disturbances. They don't replace the wiring path that lets a breaker disconnect a fault.

Bonding connects metal parts

Bonding provides a path back to the electrical source if a live wire touches an appliance frame or other metal part. That path helps a breaker or fuse shut off the fault.

For example, a damaged wire can energize a washing machine's metal case. The equipment grounding connection helps the protective device clear that fault. We don't rely on current traveling through the soil to trip the breaker.

What we check

  • The grounding electrodes and connections. We look for damage, corrosion, loose clamps, and changes to the system.
  • Metal water piping and other required bonding. Plumbing changes can interrupt existing connections.
  • The panel and equipment connections. We check where neutral and grounding connections belong for that installation.
  • Added equipment. A generator, spa, hot tub, or new circuit can need particular grounding and bonding arrangements.

We'll explain any problem we find and the work to correct it. The equipment, existing installation, and applicable local requirements determine the details.

Common things that need a closer look

Plumbing has changed

A plastic replacement pipe can change a path that an older electrical system relied on. We check the whole grounding arrangement instead of assuming the old clamp still does its job.

An inspection mentions missing bonding

Send us the wording from the inspection report. We'll check what it refers to and explain the correction. You don't need to interpret the electrical terminology first.

You're adding a generator

The generator's neutral arrangement and transfer equipment determine the grounding and bonding setup. An extra ground rod is not a universal fix. We check the system before connecting it.

You're installing a hot tub or spa

Water and nearby metal make the details especially important. We check the equipment instructions and the installation requirements before connecting power.

What affects the cost?

We need to see what already exists, which connections need work, and how we can reach them. If you're replacing a panel, we include the grounding and bonding work that the upgrade requires in the scope.

Don't ignore a shock or tingling sensation

Stop using the affected equipment and keep others away. Don't test it by touching it again. Call an electrician; a small shock can signal a serious fault. Keep panel and equipment covers in place.
