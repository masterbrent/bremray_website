Keep the essentials running, even when you're away

A standby generator can keep your furnace, fridge, sump pump, and other selected equipment running during an outage. We size the system around what you need, then check the installation site and fuel supply.

The generator starts and transfers power automatically. It still takes time to start and switch over, so it doesn't replace a battery backup for equipment that cannot tolerate an interruption.

What will it cost?

The generator is only part of the job. The transfer switch, wiring route, fuel connection, site preparation, permits, and any electrical changes all affect the estimate.

How much of the house do you want to power?

The essentials

You may mainly need the furnace, refrigerator, sump pump, well pump, lights, and a few outlets. We check each load, including the extra power motors need when they start.

More of the home

Central air, electric cooking, and other large appliances change the sizing. We can compare a larger generator with a system that manages selected loads.

There's no single generator size for a particular house size. Your equipment and how you want to use it determine the right capacity.

Natural gas or propane

If you have natural gas

A gas connection avoids tank refills while the supply remains available. We check the selected generator's fuel demand and coordinate any gas work the installation needs.

If you use propane

Tank size, generator demand, weather, and the equipment you run affect how long the fuel lasts. We coordinate the installation requirements with the propane supplier. Plan for refills during an extended outage.

What the installation involves

  1. Check the site. We review your panel, fuel supply, and a location that meets the generator's clearance requirements and local rules.
  2. Agree on the scope. We explain the generator, transfer equipment, preparation, electrical work, and gas work in the estimate.
  3. Prepare and install. We coordinate the base, delivery, wiring, transfer switch, fuel connection, and required permits and inspections.
  4. Test and walk you through it. We check operation and explain the controls, maintenance, and what to do during an outage.

The schedule depends on the site, equipment availability, fuel work, and inspections. We'll discuss those details when we plan your installation.

What the transfer switch does

The transfer switch separates generator power from utility power. It connects the home to the generator after startup, then returns it to utility power when the outage ends.

If we include load management, the system can hold back selected appliances when necessary. We'll explain what that means for the equipment you want to run.

Plan for maintenance

A standby generator needs regular service even if you rarely use it. Oil, filters, the battery, and scheduled test runs all matter. We explain the schedule for your model.

During a long outage, follow the manual's shutdown and inspection instructions. Generac's air-cooled models require checks during extended use; automatic operation doesn't remove that responsibility.

We install Generac standby generators. Bremray is a Generac Aligned Partner. Warranty coverage depends on the model and its current terms.

Would a portable generator be enough?

If you're comfortable setting it up and switching over manually, a portable generator with an interlock may suit your needs. You can also compare both options.
