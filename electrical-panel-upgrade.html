Why do panels need to be replaced?

A panel may need replacement because it has damaged or unreliable equipment, or because it can't accommodate the work you need. Those are different problems. We check the cause before choosing a solution.

1. The breakers aren't doing their job

A breaker should cut the power when a circuit carries more current than its wiring can handle. If it doesn't, the wiring can overheat and start a fire.

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco breakers have failed to trip in overload tests. Some Zinsco panels have red or blue breaker handles, but we identify the equipment by its label, not color alone.

Older fuse boxes need a different check. Oversized or bypassed fuses can leave the wiring unprotected. We'll check for those problems, damage, and whether the box can handle your needs.

2. You're asking more of your electrical system

An EV charger, heat pump, or other new equipment can add more demand than your existing service can handle. A breaker that keeps tripping may also point to a problem on one circuit. We'll find out what's causing the problem and calculate how much power your home needs.

3. Is a larger service necessary?

Replacing a panel doesn't always mean increasing the amount of power coming into your home.

Intelligent load management can control how much power certain equipment uses at one time. For example, a compatible EV charger can slow down while other appliances are running, then charge faster when more power is available.

That can sometimes avoid a service upgrade. We'll compare the options for your home before recommending a larger service. Load management won't fix a damaged panel or unreliable breakers.

  • What happens: Which breakers trip, what loses power, and when it happens

What does a panel upgrade involve?

We check more than the box. The existing wiring, service equipment, and work you're planning determine what needs to change.

  • A suitable panel and compatible breakers
  • The grounding and bonding work the installation needs
  • Required disconnects, circuit protection, and surge protection
  • Connections tightened to the manufacturer's specifications
  • Electrical permits, inspections, and utility coordination where needed

Michigan has separate requirements for residential and other electrical work. We check the rules that apply to your address and project. A list of changes in a newer code edition doesn't mean every existing home needs all of them.
