Choose the charging speed you need

A bigger charger won't make every car charge faster. Your vehicle sets its own limit, and your daily mileage matters more than the largest number on the box.

Level 1: a regular outlet

Level 1 uses 120 volts. It can suit a plug-in hybrid or a driver who covers fewer miles and has plenty of time to charge. The outlet and circuit still need to suit the charger; don't assume an old garage outlet is ready for it.

Level 2: faster home charging

Level 2 uses a 240-volt circuit. Many drivers choose it so they can put back the day's driving overnight. Charging time depends on your vehicle, charger settings, and how much charge you need.

Tell us the model and year

Charging limits can change between model years and trims. We check the actual vehicle and charger before sizing the circuit. That applies whether you drive a Tesla, Ford, Chevy, Rivian, or another EV.

If you plan to add a second EV later, mention it now. We can compare the cost of preparing for it with what you need today.

Hardwired charger or plug-in charger?

A hardwired charger connects directly to the circuit. A plug-in charger uses a matching outlet. Either can work well when the equipment and installation suit the location.

  • Already have a charger? Give us the model so we can check its installation requirements.
  • Park outside? We use equipment and connections rated for outdoor use.
  • Want a plug-in setup? We check the outlet type, circuit, and required protection. A 240-volt outlet isn't automatically the right one.
  • Still choosing? We can talk through cable reach, connector compatibility, scheduling, and load management before you buy.

We install home charging equipment including Tesla Wall Connectors, ChargePoint, and Wallbox. The right choice depends on your car and home.

Will you need a panel upgrade?

Not necessarily. We calculate the power your home needs before recommending an upgrade. The panel's amp rating and the number of empty breaker spaces don't tell the whole story.

A compatible load management system can reduce charging while other appliances use more power. That can sometimes let you add a charger without increasing your electrical service.

For two EVs, we can look at compatible chargers that share available power. We'll check the manufacturer's requirements for the exact setup.

What happens during installation?

  1. We review the job. Your vehicle, parking spot, panel location, and plans for future charging help us work out the scope.
  2. We explain the work and price. We identify the wiring route, any electrical changes, and what the estimate includes.
  3. We install and check the charger. We handle the electrical permit and inspection arrangements and show you how to use the setup.

A finished basement, detached garage, long wiring route, or underground work can add time. Tell us about those when you send your request.

Common questions

Should I install a larger circuit for my next car?

Tell us what you might buy next. We can compare preparing the wiring now with keeping this installation smaller. A larger circuit only helps if your home and charging equipment can use it.

Can I use the dryer outlet?

Ask us to check it first. The outlet, wiring, circuit rating, and charger must match. Sharing a circuit may need approved load management equipment. Don't assume a plug adapter makes it suitable.

Can you install a charger in a detached garage?

Yes. We check the power already available in the garage and the route for any new wiring. Underground work can affect the price.

Do I need to buy the charger first?

No. Tell us about your car and where you park. We can help you choose before you buy, or install suitable equipment you already own.
